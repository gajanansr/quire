# Quire — Privacy Policy

**Last updated:** 13 September 2026
**Applies to:** Quire for Android (`app.quire.android`), all versions.

---

## The short version

Quire collects nothing about you. It has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising,
no crash reporting, and no server of its own. Your books, your place in them, the
passages you save and the minutes you read stay on your phone.

This is not only a promise. **Quire does not hold the `INTERNET` permission**, so
Android will not let it open a network connection at all — not for telemetry, not
for updates, not by accident through a library it depends on. See
*[Why "no network" is stronger than a promise](#why-no-network-is-stronger-than-a-promise)*.

## What Quire collects

Nothing. There is no sign-up, no login, no profile, no device identifier, no
advertising ID, and no usage or diagnostic data of any kind sent anywhere.

Quire has no way to know that you exist.

## What Quire stores, and where

All of it is on your device, inside Quire's own private app storage, which no other
app can read:

| What | Why it is kept |
|---|---|
| A copy of each book you import (EPUB, PDF or TXT) | So a book stays readable after you move or delete the original file |
| The text extracted from each book, and its chapters | So the book can be reflowed, searched by chapter and paginated |
| A cover image, taken from the book itself | Shown in your library |
| Your place in each book | So it opens where you left it |
| Bookmarks and highlighted passages | The list under Bookmarks |
| Minutes read per day, your reading streak and daily goal | The habit screens |
| Your settings: theme, typeface, text size, justification | So the app looks the way you left it |

Two things follow from that:

- **Uninstalling Quire deletes all of it.** There is no copy anywhere else, and no
  way to recover it. Keep your original book files if they matter to you.
- **None of it is backed up.** Quire sets `android:allowBackup="false"`, so Android's
  automatic backup does not copy your library to Google Drive.

## What Quire never does

- No account, no login, no email address collected.
- No analytics, product telemetry, or usage measurement.
- No advertising, and no advertising or tracking SDK in the app.
- No crash or error reporting sent off the device.
- No location, contacts, camera, microphone, photo library or health data — Quire
  does not request any of those permissions, and a test in the codebase fails the
  build if one ever appears.
- No sale or sharing of anything, because there is nothing to sell or share.
- No cloud sync.

## Permissions Quire holds, and why

This is the complete list, taken from the manifest that is actually packaged into
the release build. There are five, and none of them can move a byte off your phone.

| Permission | Why |
|---|---|
| `WAKE_LOCK` | Keeps the CPU awake while a large book is being processed, so importing a long PDF survives the screen turning off |
| `FOREGROUND_SERVICE` | Backs that same import work when Android runs it as expedited |
| `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` | Lets an interrupted import resume after a restart instead of leaving a half-imported book |
| `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE` | Read-only. Requested by Android's WorkManager library, which inspects it for every background job it schedules. It reveals whether a network exists; it cannot use one |
| `app.quire.android.DYNAMIC_RECEIVER_NOT_EXPORTED_PERMISSION` | Generated by Android itself. It stops other apps from sending messages to Quire's internal broadcast receivers |

**Quire does not hold `INTERNET`**, and it holds no storage permission either: books
arrive through Android's own file picker, which hands Quire one file you chose and
nothing else.

### Why "no network" is stronger than a promise

Every Android app's permission list is assembled from its own manifest *and* from
every library it uses. Libraries can add `INTERNET` for their own reasons — a
logging or diagnostics backend bundled several levels down, for instance — and the
app's author may never notice.

Quire explicitly removes `INTERNET` from the merged result. An automated test
(`NoNetworkPermissionTest`) reads the packaged manifest back on every build and fails
if the permission reappears, or if any permission appears that has not been reviewed.
With the permission absent, the operating system itself refuses the connection,
whatever any part of the app might intend.

## The two moments anything leaves your device

Both require you to tap something, and in both cases Quire hands off to an app you
pick — it never sends anything itself.

**1. Share.** When you share a passage, a reading streak or a book, Quire builds
either a piece of text or a single PNG image and passes it to Android's share
chooser. You choose where it goes. The image is written to Quire's temporary cache,
and the app you pick is granted permission to read that one file, for that one
hand-off; the folder is emptied the next time you share. The envelope contains the
passage and the book it came from, and no identifier of any kind — a test asserts
exactly that.

**Save** writes the same image to your device's Pictures folder, in a `Quire` album,
where it is yours like any other photo.

**Copy** puts the passage on your clipboard. Android may show a notification that an
app copied something; that is the system, not Quire.

**2. The author's site.** The line at the foot of Settings opens
`https://gajananrathod.in` in your browser. From that moment you are on that site and
its privacy policy applies, not this one. Quire — having no network permission — could
not send anything there even if it wanted to. It only asks Android to open a web
address.

If a website, source-code or contact link is shown in Settings in a future version,
it works the same way: Quire asks your browser or mail app to open an address.

## Children

Quire is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any age.

## Your rights

There is no personal data held by the author or by anyone else, so there is nothing
to request, export, correct or delete. The data on your own device is entirely under
your control: remove a bookmark in the app, or uninstall Quire to erase everything it
has stored.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it, and the updated version
replaces this one at the same URL.

## Contact

Questions about this policy, or anything Quire does with your books:

GitHub, which is public and monitored.
